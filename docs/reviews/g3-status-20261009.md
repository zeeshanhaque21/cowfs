## What the harness measures

`bench/pjdfstest.py` builds the pinned pjdfstest (`85a8aea9e685999ef0540392fd80535f873d7ff7`) and runs one case list on two arms of one host.
Native arm: a directory on APFS.
cowfs arm: a private core store served over the macOS NFS loopback, under the run's own temp dir.
Each case keeps its raw TAP stream and sha256.
`identity.json` is written while the mount is up and must show two different `st_dev` values, an `nfs` mount at the run's own path, and no `problem`.
Without a valid identity no case runs (INVALID, exit 3).
Assertions are paired by what the pinned script proves, never by text or bare position.
Exit: 0 PASS, 1 FAIL, 2 UNMEASURABLE, 3 INVALID.
Root-required assertions (`-u`, `-g`, `mknod`, `not root`) cannot run as the non-root user on either arm.
They are counted and excluded, never scored.

## The bar

"No worse than native" means: no established assertion that passes natively and fails on the mount, other than listed by-design divergences.
Native failing and cowfs passing is never scored as a win.
A harness PASS with only coverage reasons is NOT acceptance.
The small sample below shows why.

## Cowfs worse than native and not by-design

One case, two scripts: `ftruncate/12.t` #2 and `truncate/12.t` #2.
Native passes, cowfs fails, reproduced in a second run (`20261009T010616Z`, 2 ok and 1 not ok per script on cowfs).
The script (`tests/ftruncate/12.t` lines 14 to 21) truncates a file to 999999999999999 bytes and accepts only `EFBIG`, `EINVAL` or `0`.
Any other answer prints a bare `not ok 2` with no operation text, which is why this sat in the textless bucket and not in E.
Mechanism is inferred from source, the errno was not observed on the wire: `crates/cowfs-core/src/file.rs` sets `MAX_FILE` to 1 << 42 (4 TiB), `crates/cowfs-core/src/io.rs` line 161 answers `Error::NoSpace` for a larger size, and that maps to `ENOSPC`.
The earlier "timestamp order" hypothesis for these two is withdrawn: the script has no timestamp check.
Not fixed here.
A correct fix needs a distinct file-too-big error through `cowfs-vfs`, `cowfs-core`, the NFS translate table and the FUSE mapping, which is more than a small defect.

Sweep of every worse position by case (native ok, cowfs not ok, 52 cases):
- 38 cases have `mkfifo`, `bind` or `mknod` EIO rows, so their other worse rows are #107 cascades.
- 13 cases are the #108 `pathconf` cases, 2 of which (`mkfifo/03.t`, `mknod/03.t`) also have EIO rows.
- `unlink/14.t` #4 is the silly-rename case.
- `ftruncate/12.t` and `truncate/12.t` are the only cases with none of the above (38 + 13 - 2 + 3 = 52).

Limits on that statement:
- Cascade attribution inside the 38 cases is by case, not by operation text, and 8116 assertions cannot be paired and are not adjudicated.
- The native oracle fails 3733 non-root assertions itself, so those are outside the gate.
